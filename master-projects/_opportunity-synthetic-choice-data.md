    <h4 id="synthetic-choice-data">Generating Synthetic Transport Choice Datasets with Behaviourally Guided LLMs and Generative Models</h4>
    <p>
      Collecting discrete choice experiment data to estimate travel behaviour models is often costly, time-consuming, and difficult to scale for emerging transport policy questions. While Large Language Models (LLMs) show promise in generating synthetic survey responses, standard prompting based solely on socio-demographic attributes tends to produce overly homogeneous choices that fail to reflect real-world population diversity. To overcome this limitation, synthetic traveller personas can be enriched with explicit behavioural guidance, such as travel attitudes, comfort preferences, and willingness to trade travel time for cost. Grounding persona prompts in behavioural theory provides a structured way to inject realistic taste heterogeneity into synthetic choice data.
    </p>
    <p>
      In this MSc thesis, you will design and evaluate a pipeline to generate naturalistic synthetic transport choice datasets using LLMs. You will construct diverse synthetic traveller personas by combining socio-demographic profiles with explicit behavioural assumptions across hypothetical transport scenarios. Beyond LLM personas, this project invites you to explore other generative AI models, such as Conditional Variational Autoencoders (C-VAEs) or Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), to synthesise realistic choice patterns. You will evaluate the generated data in terms of statistical fidelity, diversity, and behavioural plausibility. Ultimately, your findings will help establish reliable generative methodologies for creating synthetic travel choice data to support transport planning and policymaking.
    </p>
    <p><strong>Supervision:</strong> Gabriel Nova (TPM, TU Delft)</p>
