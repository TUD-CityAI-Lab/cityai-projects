    <h4 id="delphos-llm">LLM-Enhanced Delphos: Accelerating Reinforcement Learning for Choice Model Specification</h4>
    <p>
      Specifying discrete choice models traditionally requires substantial expert trial-and-error to balance goodness-of-fit with behavioural plausibility. While Delphos frames this task as a sequential decision-making problem using reinforcement learning, training such agents from scratch demands extensive trial estimations and struggles with cross-dataset transfer. Drawing inspiration from recent advances in <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00282" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">LLM-enhanced reinforcement learning</a>, Large Language Models offer rich semantic reasoning and contextual domain knowledge that can accelerate agent exploration. By integrating LLMs into the Delphos pipeline, we can equip the agent to interpret estimation feedback and guide model specification more effectively.
    </p>
    <p>
      In this MSc thesis, you will design and evaluate an LLM-augmented reinforcement learning pipeline for automated choice model specification. You will explore complementary roles for LLMs, investigating how they can act as reward interpreters assessing behavioural plausibility and as action translators generating concrete Apollo utility functions. In addition, you will assess whether semantic prompting enhances sample efficiency and facilitates transferring learned modelling strategies across diverse transport datasets. Ultimately, your research will advance sample-efficient, autonomous AI assistants that bridge machine learning and behavioural modelling.
    </p>
    <p><strong>Supervision:</strong> Gabriel Nova (TPM, TU Delft)</p>
